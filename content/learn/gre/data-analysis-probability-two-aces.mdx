<Question>
A standard deck of $52$ playing cards contains $4$ aces. If two cards are drawn at random without replacement, what is the probability that both cards are aces?
</Question>

<Choices answer="A">
<Choice label="A">$\frac{1}{221}$</Choice>
<Choice label="B">$\frac{1}{169}$</Choice>
<Choice label="C">$\frac{1}{52}$</Choice>
<Choice label="D">$\frac{1}{26}$</Choice>
<Choice label="E">$\frac{1}{13}$</Choice>
</Choices>
</QuestionPane>

<ExplanationPane>
<Explanation>
<SolutionStep number={1} title="Find the probability the first card is an ace">
There are $4$ aces out of $52$ cards, so $P(\text{first ace}) = \frac{4}{52} = \frac{1}{13}$.
</SolutionStep>

<SolutionStep number={2} title="Find the probability the second card is an ace, given the first was">
After removing one ace, $3$ aces remain among $51$ cards: $P(\text{second ace}) = \frac{3}{51} = \frac{1}{17}$.
</SolutionStep>

<SolutionStep number={3} title="Multiply the probabilities">
$P(\text{both aces}) = \frac{1}{13} × \frac{1}{17} = \frac{1}{221}$, choice **A**.
</SolutionStep>
</Explanation>
</ExplanationPane>
