<Question>
A chemist has Solution A, which is $30\%$ acid, and Solution B, which is $60\%$ acid. How many liters of Solution B must be mixed with Solution A to create $30$ liters of a solution that is $50\%$ acid?
</Question>

<Choices answer="B">
<Choice label="A">15</Choice>
<Choice label="B">20</Choice>
<Choice label="C">22.5</Choice>
<Choice label="D">25</Choice>
<Choice label="E">27</Choice>
</Choices>
</QuestionPane>

<ExplanationPane>
<Explanation>
<SolutionStep number={1} title="Set up the acid-content equation">
Let $x$ be the liters of Solution A, so $30 - x$ is the liters of Solution B. The total acid content equation is $0.30x + 0.60(30 - x) = 0.50(30)$.
</SolutionStep>

<SolutionStep number={2} title="Simplify and solve for x">
$0.30x + 18 - 0.60x = 15$, so $-0.30x = -3$, giving $x = 10$.
</SolutionStep>

<SolutionStep number={3} title="Find the amount of Solution B">
Solution B $= 30 - x = 30 - 10 = 20$ liters.
</SolutionStep>
</Explanation>
</ExplanationPane>
