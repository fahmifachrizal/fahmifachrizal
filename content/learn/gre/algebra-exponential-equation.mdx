<Question>
If $2^{x+1} = 3 \cdot 2^{x-1} + 4$, what is the value of $x$?
</Question>

<Choices answer="C">
<Choice label="A">1</Choice>
<Choice label="B">2</Choice>
<Choice label="C">3</Choice>
<Choice label="D">4</Choice>
<Choice label="E">5</Choice>
</Choices>
</QuestionPane>

<ExplanationPane>
<Explanation>
<SolutionStep number={1} title="Rewrite both sides in terms of 2^x">
$2^{x+1} = 2 \cdot 2^x$, and $3 \cdot 2^{x-1} = \frac{3}{2} \cdot 2^x$. The equation becomes $2 \cdot 2^x = \frac{3}{2} \cdot 2^x + 4$.
</SolutionStep>

<SolutionStep number={2} title="Isolate the 2^x term">
Subtracting $\frac{3}{2} \cdot 2^x$ from both sides: $\left(2 - \frac{3}{2}\right) \cdot 2^x = 4$, so $\frac{1}{2} \cdot 2^x = 4$.
</SolutionStep>

<SolutionStep number={3} title="Solve for 2^x, then x">
Multiplying both sides by 2: $2^x = 8 = 2^3$, so $x = 3$.
</SolutionStep>

<SolutionStep number={4} title="Verify">
Substituting $x = 3$: $2^4 = 16$ and $3 \cdot 2^2 + 4 = 12 + 4 = 16$. Both sides match, confirming $x = 3$, choice **C**.
</SolutionStep>
</Explanation>
</ExplanationPane>
</QuestionLayout>
