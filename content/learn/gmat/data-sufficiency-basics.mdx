<Question>
What is the value of $x$?

1. $x + 3 = 7$
2. $x^2 = 16$
</Question>

<Choices answer="A">
<Choice label="A">Statement (1) ALONE is sufficient, but statement (2) alone is not sufficient</Choice>
<Choice label="B">Statement (2) ALONE is sufficient, but statement (1) alone is not sufficient</Choice>
<Choice label="C">BOTH statements TOGETHER are sufficient, but NEITHER statement alone is sufficient</Choice>
<Choice label="D">EACH statement ALONE is sufficient</Choice>
<Choice label="E">Statements (1) and (2) TOGETHER are not sufficient</Choice>
</Choices>
</QuestionPane>

<ExplanationPane>
<Explanation>
<SolutionStep number={1} title="Evaluate statement (1) alone">
$x + 3 = 7$ gives exactly one value: $x = 4$. A single linear equation with one unknown and one solution is sufficient on its own.
</SolutionStep>

<SolutionStep number={2} title="Evaluate statement (2) alone">
$x^2 = 16$ has **two** possible solutions: $x = 4$ or $x = -4$. Since the value of $x$ isn't uniquely determined, statement (2) alone is not sufficient.
</SolutionStep>

<SolutionStep number={3} title="Draw the conclusion">
Statement (1) alone pins down a unique value of $x$, while statement (2) alone does not. That matches choice **A**.
</SolutionStep>
</Explanation>
</ExplanationPane>
